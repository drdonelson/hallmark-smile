Overview

What This Is

A patient-facing AI smile simulator embedded on dental practice websites. Patient takes a selfie in the dental chair; the simulator generates a photorealistic "after" showing a full cosmetic makeover — Dr. Apa–style golden proportion symmetry, individually defined teeth with visible embrasures, BL1 whitening. Goal: patient emotionally commits to treatment before leaving the chair.

Business model

  • SaaS via Lucid ROI platform
  • White-labeled embed via ?theme= param
  • Lead capture → dentist dashboard
  • Per-tenant usage metering (R2-backed)

Competing product

  • bitebot.io — same category
  • Quality gap has closed significantly (July 2026)
  • Closing the remaining gap is the primary technical goal

Competitive Analysis

What Bitebot Does Differently

We've compared bitebot's before/after pairs extensively. Their output shows consistent signals of full lower-face regeneration, not tooth inpainting:

Signal in bitebot outputWhat it implies
Hair and upper face are pixel-identical to before NOT doing full-face AI — upper face is preserved
Smile is noticeably wider than original anatomy allows Regenerating more than just teeth — perioral zone is included
Lip shape and support change between before/after Lower face (lips, gum, perioral) is regenerated as a unit
No visible composite seam anywhere Edit zone boundary falls on cheek skin, outside the face expression area
Every case gets full arch canine-to-canine Not constrained by original tooth detection — fills full ellipse
July 2026 status: Our GPT full-image + tooth composite primary path is now competitive with bitebot on normal and moderate-damage cases. The outer-lip composite mask gives us dentally correct anatomy (full gingival emergence, intact incisal edges). Remaining gap: GPT mouth drift on edge cases, and the quality:medium ceiling imposed by the Cloudflare Worker 30s limit.
Iteration History

Full Iteration History — 16 Attempts

Every approach tried, from first commit to now — what we learned from each.

01

Brightness-threshold masking

Dead

Built buildToothMask() using HSV/brightness to find white pixels. Finds ~628 pixels. Misses yellowish, stained, decayed teeth entirely. Never use — buildMediaPipeMask supersedes it completely (finds 6,000+ pixels geometrically regardless of tooth color).

02

ControlNet + LoRA full-face inpaint (Modal A10G)

Dead

SDXL + dental LoRA (456MB, trained on ~600 Instagram before/after pairs). Ran at 1024px full-face — tooth region only ~200px wide. Cannot render individual tooth anatomy at 200px. Additional failure: LoRA img2img had no workable strength range (≥0.70 → white slab, <0.70 → too subtle). Modal/RunPod defunded June 2026. LoRA weights on HuggingFace (drdonelson/dental-lora).

03

FLUX Pro Fill crop-zoom

Fallback 2

Crop mouth region, zoom to 1024px, FLUX inpaint. Mask convention: WHITE=edit (opposite of Ideogram). Decent results but Ideogram v2 consistently outperforms on tooth anatomy detail. Now Fallback 2.

04

Ideogram v2 crop-zoom

Fallback 1

Crop to tooth region, scale to 1024px. Ideogram v2 via Replicate. Was primary through June 2026; now Fallback 1 behind GPT composite. Core discoveries still live in the fallback path:

  • Tooth erase — fill MP-detected teeth with rgb(20,12,12) before sending. Without this, Ideogram barely changes existing light teeth → white slab with no embrasures.
  • Mask convention — Ideogram v2 uses BLACK=edit, WHITE=preserve. FLUX/SDXL are the opposite. Getting this backwards silently edits the face.
  • Bib desaturation — patients wear teal bibs. Ideogram samples context → blue teeth. Fix: desaturate all pixels below the tooth zone.
  • Pixel-precise blend mask — bbox rectangle → visible rectangular seam. MediaPipe mask brightness→alpha → blend follows exact tooth geometry.
  • Commissure fade — fade blend to 0 over outer 5–10% of tooth width (Ideogram drifts at mouth corners).
  • destination-in alpha trap — clips by source ALPHA not color. Blend mask canvas must default transparent.
05

Lighting harmonization (harmonizeTeeth())

Live (Fallback path)

After compositing Ideogram teeth, they looked "pasted on" — too cool, too bright. Built LAB color space illuminant transfer: sample scene color temperature from perioral skin only (warm pixels: LAB a > 4). Transfer ~half the chroma shift, match brightness to skin highlight level, apply per-column light falloff. Still active in the Ideogram fallback path. Not needed for GPT primary — full-image generation handles lighting natively.

06

Collapsed/edentulous detection

Live

Full-arch tooth loss → nearly closed mouth → inner-lip bbox aspect <0.18 (normal smiles: 0.22–0.26). When collapsed: tight 0.5×/1.0× padding, vertical mask expansion, perioralSmile prompt, harmonization disabled.

07

Perioral ellipse for edentulous cases

Live (Fallback path)

Tooth-confined inpainting can't restore lip support — bounded by original lip line. Fix: draw perioral ellipse (rx=0.72·tbW, ry=2.2·tbH) covering full mouth opening including lips. Ideogram regenerates entire smile zone coherently. 5/5 validated. Shipped June 2026.

08

Best-of-2 QC

Live (Fallback path)

Ideogram has large run-to-run variance. Run 2 parallel draws, score both with scoreToothGeneration() (embrasure local-minima + tonal stddev − cold glare penalty + coverage fill ratio), keep higher score. ~$0.16/run vs $0.08.

09

Perioral ellipse for ALL cases (attempt 1)

Reverted

Applied perioral ellipse to all patients with rx=0.85·tbW, ry=1.6·tbH. On patients with intact existing teeth, Ideogram generated a grotesquely wide, anatomically impossible "clown smile." Reverted within hours while live on Seven Bridges Dental. Root cause: ry=1.6 cuts through the smile arc — vertical opening too small relative to horizontal, creating an unnatural mouth shape that Ideogram can't resolve plausibly.

10

Hires geometry — tight crop from full-res photo

Live (Fallback path)

Production crop (2.0×/1.5× padding) spans nearly the full image → teeth hit Ideogram at ~205px, no real zoom benefit. Switch to: crop from the full-resolution original (not the downscaled working image), tight 0.5×/1.0× padding, ~3:1 AR matching Ideogram's native output. Teeth now ~500px wide at Ideogram — dramatically sharper anatomy.

11

Perioral ellipse for ALL cases (attempt 2)

Live (Fallback path)

Re-applied perioral approach to all cases with better calibration: rx=0.85·tbW, ry=2.5·tbH (larger vertical than the failed attempt's 1.6), openSmile prompt. Larger ry prevents the ellipse from cutting through the smile arc. Validated across 7 case types. No clown-face artifacts.

12

Severe damage routing (fullArch variant)

Live (Fallback path)

Measure average brightness of original tooth pixels pre-erase. If avgBright <90 → route to fullArch Ideogram variant. The normalSmile prompt negated "wider smile, different smile width" — exactly wrong for heavily decayed teeth. fullArch prompt explicitly requests "premolars visible toward the corners" and "broad confident smile arc." GPT primary handles severe cases natively now, making this a fallback guard.

13

Dark gum band fix — MP-only blend mask

Live (Fallback path)

After attempt 11, the Ideogram fallback was showing a visible dark band at the gumline. Root cause: the blend mask included the perioral ellipse expansion zone, so Ideogram's generated (darker) gum composited over the original face above the teeth. Fix: use the MP-only tooth mask for blending, regardless of what the edit mask is. Result: original gum shows naturally above the generated teeth, no dark band artifact.

14

Cant correction + gingival emergence fix

Live (Fallback path)

Two problems found on the same patient, fixed together:

  • Canted occlusal plane — Ideogram follows head tilt in the crop → generated teeth not parallel to the interpupillary line. Fix: compute tilt angle from eye corner landmarks (indices 33, 133, 362, 263), rotate the crop canvas by -tiltAngle before the Ideogram call (and rotate all mask draws the same way). Threshold: <0.5° skipped.
  • Dark at gingival emergence — for the perioral path, Ideogram regenerates clean pink gum tissue, but blending only the tooth pixels left the original (darker) gum visible above the crowns. Fix: expand the blend mask 12% of tbH upward for perioral cases, bringing the new gum-tooth junction into the composite.
15

GPT full-image + tooth composite — CURRENT PRIMARY

Primary (July 2026)

The breakthrough that closed the bitebot quality gap on normal and severe cases. Core insight: gpt-image-1 on the FULL face produces dramatically better teeth realism AND automatic lighting integration — full-face context gives GPT everything needed to generate teeth that belong to the face.

  • The catch: GPT re-renders the entire image, beautifying/de-aging the whole face. Shipping GPT whole-image gives patients a photo of a younger, different person. Unacceptable.
  • The fix: Generate with GPT, composite only the mouth back onto the untouched original face. Affine warp corrects GPT's mouth drift before compositing.
  • Mask is the whole game: Use the OUTER lip polygon (grown ~16% + feathered 18% of mouth width). The inner-lip mask clips GPT's gingival emergence and incisal edges — a dentist WILL catch this. The blend must land on perioral skin, not across the teeth.
  • Cost: ~$0.04/sim at quality:medium — cheaper than Ideogram's $0.08.
  • 30s limit: quality:high exceeds the Cloudflare Worker 30s subrequest limit. Production uses medium + 40s AbortController → Ideogram fallback. High quality requires a longer-timeout backend.

Validated on 11 real office photos. Shipped to production July 2026.

16

Kling 2.5 Turbo Pro video upgrade

Live

Video model upgraded from Seedance v1 lite (weak motion) to fal-ai/kling-video/v2.5-turbo/pro/image-to-video. Best-in-class human-motion smile/laugh reveals, matching bitebot's video quality tier. Natural reveal→laugh, identity preserved, teeth stable. 5s ~99s / 10s ~73s queue time. ~$0.35 for 5s.

Current State

Current Quality Assessment

Working well (July 2026)

  • Normal/mild discoloration — GPT primary delivers natural lighting integration
  • Severe missing/broken teeth — GPT full-image handles these; crop-zoom couldn't
  • Edentulous/collapsed cases
  • Canted occlusal plane (cant correction active in Ideogram fallback)
  • Gingival emergence (outer-lip mask in GPT; perioral expansion in fallback)
  • Video — Kling 2.5 Turbo Pro is bitebot-tier quality
  • Lead capture + email + multi-tenant SaaS infrastructure

Still short of bitebot

  • GPT mouth drift on edge cases → falls to Ideogram (lower quality)
  • quality:medium ceiling (high quality blocked by 30s Worker limit)
  • Perioral skin halo — thin ring of GPT's smoother skin around lips (soft, acceptable)
  • Ideogram fallback run-to-run variance (~15% bad draws even with best-of-2)
  • Unknown if bitebot uses fine-tuned model — we can't see their stack

Honest Ceiling Assessment

The GPT primary path is near bitebot quality on the majority of cases. The remaining gap is: (1) cases where GPT mouth drift triggers the alignment guard and we fall to Ideogram, (2) the anatomy detail improvement available from quality:high, and (3) whatever edge bitebot may have from a fine-tuned model. Without training data, we are at or very near the ceiling of zero-shot generation. The path to definitively beating bitebot on all cases is FLUX Kontext LoRA fine-tuning (see roadmap).

Critical Worker Constraints

Cloudflare Worker 30-second subrequest limit. gpt-image-1 quality:medium finishes within this window. quality:high does not — observed timeout at exactly 300s client-side. Ideogram takes 20–90s and is handled async: Worker starts the Replicate prediction → returns prediction ID → browser polls /api/replicate/status?id=X every 4s. Same async pattern for Kling video.

GPT Composite Specifics

  • Model: gpt-image-1 at quality:medium (production)
  • Mask convention: transparent (alpha=0) = edit zone, opaque (alpha=255) = preserve. But GPT ignores this mask for generation — only the composite step matters.
  • Always route through POST /api/gpt/edit?tenant=<slug> — this path meters against the tenant cap. The generic /v1/images/edits passthrough is unmetered and will burn spend past quota.
  • Affine guard: reject and fall to Ideogram if scale <0.55 or >1.8, or mouth-center shift >12% of image width
  • Cost: ~$0.04/sim at medium

Ideogram v2 Specifics (Fallback 1)

  • Mask convention: BLACK = edit zone, WHITE = preserve (opposite of FLUX/SDXL — this has burned us before)
  • Output is always ~3:1 aspect ratio regardless of input. A 5:1 input → 3:1 output with quality loss. Keep crop AR near 3:1.
  • Cost: ~$0.08/image, ~$0.16/run with best-of-2

Roadmap

Two Paths Forward

Path A — Maximizing Quality Without Training Data

Achievable with the current zero-shot stack. Estimated developer time per item:

1. GPT quality:high via longer-timeout backend — 1–3 days

Move the GPT call to Modal serverless or a Vercel edge function with a 120s timeout. Estimated +10–15% anatomy detail over medium. This is the single highest-value improvement available without training data. The Worker continues to orchestrate everything else; only the GPT call routes to the longer-timeout backend.

2. Better GPT alignment — fewer Ideogram fallbacks — 2–4 hours

Cases where GPT's mouth drift exceeds the 12% width guard fall to Ideogram (lower quality). A more explicit identity-lock prompt may reduce drift. Also: test whether the 12% guard threshold can be relaxed (15%?) while still giving acceptable composite alignment.

3. Ideogram artifact detection + auto-retry — 3–5 hours

scoreToothGeneration() already runs on both best-of-2 draws. Add a minimum score threshold — if BOTH draws score below it, run a 3rd draw. Catches ~15% of fallback runs that produce slabs or partial arches. Adds $0.08 and ~30s to those cases only.

4. Perioral skin halo reduction — 2–4 hours

The outer-lip mask brings a thin ring of GPT's slightly-smoother perioral skin. Asymmetric feathering — tighter on the upper lip/philtrum where original skin must stay, still generous at the incisal edge — would reduce this. Not yet done; current halo is soft and not a hard seam.

Path B — With Training Data (Closes the Bitebot Gap Definitively)

What we have: ~600 before/after pairs scraped from a dentist friend's Instagram (NOT commercially licensed — needs a written partner agreement). LoRA weights on HuggingFace (drdonelson/dental-lora). Dr. D's own practice archive has clean pairs (e.g., edentulous reference _DSC1529 before / _DSC3401 after).

FLUX Kontext LoRA — the right vehicle

FLUX Dev Kontext LoRA fine-tuning needs only 50–150 before/after pairs (far less than SDXL LoRA). With pairs from Dr. D's own archive, achievable without copyright risk.

  • What it would change: no mask, no crop-zoom, no bib desaturation hack, full perioral coherence, eliminates run-to-run variance
  • Training pair requirements: matched lighting, same face position, full-face photos, at least 3 case types (mild whitening, veneer makeover, full-arch restoration), 50+ pairs per type
  • Cost: ~$30–80 training on Replicate for 100 pairs/2000 steps. ~$0.06/inference.
  • Integration: add fluxKontextMakeover() as new primary; move GPT composite to Fallback 1; keep Ideogram as Fallback 2

Sprint without training data (3–5 days)

  • GPT quality:high backend migration
  • Ideogram artifact auto-retry
  • Better GPT alignment prompt
  • Perioral halo reduction

Sprint with training data (3–4 weeks)

  • Curate + license 50+ pairs from Dr. D's archive
  • Fine-tune FLUX Kontext LoRA on Replicate
  • A/B test vs current GPT pipeline
  • Replace primary if validated
